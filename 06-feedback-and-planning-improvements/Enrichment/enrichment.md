# Week 6 Enrichment: Feedback And Planning Improvements

This enrichment is optional. Use it when you want more context for feedback, discovery, and prioritization.

## Optional Topics

### Product Discovery

Product discovery is the work of learning what problem is worth solving before or while improving a product. It helps you decide whether feedback points to a real user need or only a nice-to-have idea.

- Read: [Product Discovery Guide by Teresa Torres](https://www.producttalk.org/product-discovery/)
- Read: [The Double Diamond: A universally accepted depiction of the design process](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/our-resources/the-double-diamond/)

### User Interviews

User interviews are structured conversations used to learn about user problems, behavior, and context. A useful interview asks about real past behavior more than imaginary future preferences.

- Read: [Nielsen Norman Group: User Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/user-interviews/)
- Watch: [Nielsen Norman Group: The 3 Types of User Interviews](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/3-types-user-interviews/)

### MVP Versus Product Market Fit

An MVP is a small product version used to learn and deliver initial value. Product market fit is a much larger signal that the product strongly satisfies a market need, so one course MVP should not be treated as proof of product market fit.

- Read: [Y Combinator: The Real Product Market Fit](https://www.ycombinator.com/library/5z-the-real-product-market-fit)
- Read: [ProductPlan: Minimum Viable Product](https://www.productplan.com/glossary/minimum-viable-product/)

### Prioritization Frameworks

Prioritization frameworks help compare possible work when there are more ideas than time. They are useful only when you still make a clear decision instead of hiding behind a score.

- Read: [ProductPlan: Product Management Prioritization Frameworks](https://www.productplan.com/learn/product-management-frameworks)

### MoSCoW Prioritization

MoSCoW groups work into must-have, should-have, could-have, and will-not-have. It is simple enough for a first improvement plan when you need to push good ideas out of the next development session.

- Read: [ProductPlan: MoSCoW Prioritization](https://www.productplan.com/glossary/moscow-prioritization/)

### Impact Versus Effort Matrix

An impact versus effort matrix compares how valuable a change might be against how hard it is to implement. It is useful when you need to choose improvements that can actually be built soon.

- Read: [LogRocket: Impact effort matrix prioritization](https://blog.logrocket.com/product-management/impact-effort-matrix-prioritization/)

### RICE Scoring

RICE scores work by reach, impact, confidence, and effort. It is more detailed than the required workflow, but it is useful when you want a more structured prioritization method.

- Read: [Intercom: RICE prioritization model](https://www.intercom.com/blog/rice-simple-prioritization-for-product-managers/)

## Stretch Activity

Create a small impact versus effort matrix for your feedback.

Add this table to `feedback-plan.md`:

```md
## Impact Versus Effort

| Improvement | Impact | Effort | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | High/Medium/Low | High/Medium/Low | Accept/Defer/Reject/Investigate |
```

Then ask Codex:

```text
Read my feedback plan and impact versus effort table.
Which selected improvements look too large for the next development session?
Which deferred ideas might be worth revisiting later?
Which decision needs a clearer reason?
```
